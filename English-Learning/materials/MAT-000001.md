## Exact Passage

A local library used to close at six in the evening. Several residents said they wanted longer opening hours, so the library began staying open until nine on Tuesdays. The change was introduced as a three-month trial rather than a permanent decision.

At first, the results seemed disappointing. Only a few people visited during the extra hours. Some staff members suggested ending the trial early. However, a librarian noticed that the new timetable appeared only on the library's website, which many regular visitors rarely checked. She suggested putting signs near the entrance and asking nearby shops to display them.

By the final month, evening visits had increased. Yet the library director was careful about what this meant. Longer hours might have helped some residents, but the increase could also reflect better publicity. Moreover, visitor numbers alone did not show whether people had found the services useful.

Before deciding what to do next, the library asked evening visitors why they had come and whether they could have visited earlier. The director wanted to understand not just how many people used the extra hours, but whom the change actually helped.
